Ella guide

The three walks from Ella

Three walks start in the town and need no vehicle. One is short, one is a photograph, and one is a proper morning with a genuine problem attached to it. All three are better before nine.

Little Adam’s Peak

The short one, and the one to do first. Steps for most of the climb, a real view from the top over the gap and the tea, and you can be back before the heat arrives.

It is also the busiest of the three after about nine in the morning, because it is the easiest and every guesthouse in town recommends it. At seven you will pass a handful of people. At ten you will queue for the rock everybody photographs from.

Nine Arches Bridge

A stone railway viaduct across a wooded valley, and the reason most people stand there is to see a train on it.

We do not print train times on this website, and that is a policy rather than an omission — the times change, published ones contradict each other, and a wrong one costs you a morning standing in the wrong place holding a phone. Ask us the day before and we will tell you what we know that day.

Two things worth knowing that do not change. First, the bridge is worth the walk whether or not one crosses while you are standing there — it is a large and slightly improbable piece of engineering in the middle of a jungle, and it is the arches that carry the photograph. Second, the crowd builds through the morning and thins in the late afternoon, and the early light is softer than the middle-of-the-day light by a wide margin. Go early for the space and stay for the arches.

Ella Rock, and the sign problem

The long one. Higher, quieter, much less signed, and the only one of the three where the walk itself is not the hard part.

Here is the problem, and it is well documented rather than rumoured. The route is partly along a railway line and partly through tea and scrub, and it is marked mostly by hand-painted arrows on rocks and walls. Those markers get moved, painted over and hidden. Signs appear pointing the wrong way. Somebody will offer to show you the path and then want paying for it, and if you decline, the next person will offer as well. There is more than one tea shop on a lesser ridge that gets presented as the top when it is not.

The defence is not complicated:

  • Download the route to your phone before you leave the town. Offline maps or a trail app — anything that works with no signal, because there is no signal for much of it. This single step removes almost the entire problem.
  • Walk it with somebody. Not for safety exactly. For the second opinion at the junction.
  • Do not accept an unrequested guide. A polite no and a continued walk is the whole technique. Nobody is going to stop you.
  • Start early. Partly the heat, partly that a path with other walkers on it is a path you cannot lose.

And the honest bit: you do not need to buy a guide from us to do this. It is walkable alone with the route downloaded, and most of our guests do exactly that. Ask us and we will tell you whether this particular week is one where we would send somebody with you — after heavy rain the ground and the visibility are different, and sometimes the answer is yes.

Which one, if you only do one

Little Adam’s Peak. It costs you two hours and a bit of effort and it gives you the view the town is famous for. Ella Rock is the better walk and the worse first choice, because if it goes wrong it takes your whole day and your temper.

If you have two mornings, do Little Adam’s Peak and the bridge on the first and Ella Rock on the second, in that order, for the same reason you learn a route before you rely on it.

In the wet months

Long trousers, socks you do not mind losing, and shoes with grip. Between about May and September the trails have leeches on them, and the people who tell you socks are enough have not always been right. There is more about this on the practicalities page, including what the wet season actually does to the view.
