Ella guide

Getting to Ella

Ella is inland, uphill, and in the middle of the island. Nothing gets here quickly, and the journey is one of the better parts of coming.

The shape of the problem

There are two ways to arrive: by road the whole way, or by road as far as the hill country and then by rail for the last stretch. That is the whole decision. Everything else is detail about which road and which day.

Whichever you choose, allow more time than the number you were given. Hill roads in Sri Lanka are slow in a way that distances do not predict, and a bus that is scheduled to take three hours is not lying to you exactly.

Coming in by train

Sri Lanka’s hill-country railway was damaged by a cyclone in November 2025, and repairs have been carried out in stages since.

We do not publish train times, routes or service status on this website. Anything we wrote would be out of date before you read it, and a wrong timetable is worse than no timetable when you are standing on a platform with a bag.

Ask us the week you travel. We will tell you what is running that week, and how the people who arrived that week actually got here — which is a more useful answer than a timetable anyway.

Ask us what’s running

By road

Three versions of the same thing, in descending order of comfort and ascending order of adventure.

A car with a driver. Door to door, your own timing, and by far the most expensive. This is the standard move for two or more people who would rather arrive with the day intact.

A tourist minibus or shared van. Fixed route, fixed departure, cheaper, and you will share it. Bookable a day or two ahead from most towns.

The public bus. Cheapest by a wide margin, no reservation, and the least comfortable ride on the island. Buses run on the roads, which is the point worth making: whatever the railway is doing, the road is there.

We are not printing fares on this page. Road prices move, they vary by how you ask and who you ask, and a stale number in print is what gets people overcharged — they quote our figure, the driver laughs, and the traveller has lost the negotiation before it started. Ask us the week you travel and we will tell you what we would pay.

From Kandy

This is the journey most people ask about, and it is the one where the honest answer is a conversation rather than a paragraph.

By road, allow half a day and be pleased if it is less. That option exists every day and does not depend on anything.

The alternative is road for the first part and rail for the last part, which is the version people come to Sri Lanka for. Whether that is the right choice this week, and where the changeover sits, depends on what is running — so we answer this one by message. It takes us thirty seconds and it saves you an afternoon of reading blog posts written in a different year.

Ask us what’s running

From Colombo and the airport

The airport is north of Colombo, so arriving on an evening flight and reaching Ella the same night is not a plan. Almost everybody breaks the journey, and breaking it in the hills is more pleasant than breaking it in Colombo.

By road it is a long day. By road and rail it is a long day with a good hour in it. Either way, the same answer as above about what is running.

From the south coast

Mirissa, Unawatuna, Tangalle, Hiriketiya. All of them are road journeys inland and then up, and all of them take most of a day, because the coast road is slow and the hill road is slower.

The common shape of a Sri Lanka trip is beach, then safari, then hills, then Kandy, and Ella is the hinge in the middle of it. If you are coming up from the coast, going on to tea country afterwards costs you almost nothing extra in travel and is the best-value extra day in the itinerary.

From Tissamaharama and the safari parks

If you have done Yala or Udawalawe you are already halfway. It is a short road journey by Sri Lankan standards and the climb at the end of it is the good part.

Plenty of people do this leg in reverse and go from here to a park for the day. That is a different thing and it lives on the tours page.

Arriving

Ella’s railway station sits in the town, and the town is one street, so there is no such thing as being far from the middle of it once you are off. Tuk-tuks wait outside. Nobody uses a meter — see the practicalities page before you agree to anything.

Our own last ten minutes are up the hillside from there, and the booking page has the route.
