The Ella guide

Ella is crowded. It is still worth two nights.

We are a hostel up the hill from the town, so read this knowing who wrote it. It is not a list of ten things you must see. It is what the place is actually like, what order to do it in, and which bits are a waste of a morning.

What the town is

Ella is one street through a gap in the hills, with guesthouses up every lane off it and a railway line running along the slope above. Mountains on both sides. It sits high enough in the hill country that the nights are cooler than the coast, which is most of the reason Sri Lankans and everyone else come up here.

It is also busy. There are more places to stay every year and they cost more every year, and between about nine in the morning and four in the afternoon there is somebody else standing at every viewpoint you were planning to stand at. Anyone describing this town as undiscovered has not been recently.

None of that ruins it. It does mean the whole trick to Ella is one thing, and here it is: be early. At seven in the morning the same ridge, the same bridge and the same path have almost nobody on them, and the light is better. By ten they are a queue. That is the single most useful sentence on this page and everything below is a consequence of it.

Who should skip it

If you have ten days in Sri Lanka and you do not want to walk uphill, Ella will be the least interesting stop on your list and you should give the nights to somewhere else. It is a walking town. The good parts of it are all at the top of something.

Two nights is the shape of it. Three if you want a full day out of the valley as well, which most people do once they get here.

Getting here

Ella is inland and uphill, and there is no fast way to it from anywhere. From Colombo or the airport, plan on losing a day to the journey and enjoy the day. From Kandy it is a half-day by road at best.

There are two shapes of journey: road the whole way, or road as far as the hill country and then the railway. Which one makes sense depends on the week you travel, and we would rather tell you by message than print something that goes out of date.

Two days in Ella, hour by hour

This is the order we would do it in, and the reasoning is entirely about heat and crowds. Every hour of it is moveable except the early starts, which are not.

Awake, probably without meaning to be. The light comes early here and so do the birds.
07:00
Little Adam’s Peak. Steps most of the way, and the shortest of the three walks from town. At this hour there will be almost nobody on it.

What time to go
11:30
The flat part of the day. This is not a gap in the plan — it is the plan. Town, a plate of rice and curry, a shower, a book, the shade. Nothing outdoors is improved between eleven and three.

Ravana Falls, or the town, depending on what the sky is doing. The falls are a tuk-tuk ride out on the main road, not a walk.

Dark, and quickly. This close to the equator the light goes in about twenty minutes and there is no long evening to sit in.

Dinner in town. Ella’s evenings are quiet and they are early — this is a town of people who have to get up at half past five.
21:00

If it rains

It will, between about May and September, and the useful thing about Ella’s rain is that it mostly comes in the afternoon. So the plan above survives the wet season almost unchanged — you do the walking before lunch and you lose the afternoon rather than the day.

What you lose in the wet months is the view rather than the walk. The ridge spends a lot of June and July inside a cloud. Some people prefer it. Nobody photographs it.

A wet afternoon is the right afternoon for the cooking class, which happens indoors and takes a few hours off your hands.

The three walks

Three walks start from the town and need no vehicle. Little Adam’s Peak is the short one, steps most of the way. Nine Arches Bridge is not really a walk, it is a timing decision. Ella Rock is the long one, and the only one where people get lost, misdirected and charged for being un-lost.

All three are better before nine. Two of them are straightforward. The third has a documented problem and is worth ten minutes of reading first.

The practical part

Ella runs mostly on cash. Tuk-tuks have no meters and the fare is agreed before you get in, not after. Data is fine in the town and unreliable in the valleys, so maps get downloaded before you walk, not on the path. And between roughly May and September there are leeches on the wet trails, which is the single most under-published fact about hiking here.

Ella, practically

Where we are in all this

We are up the hillside above the town — under ten minutes on foot to the middle of it, down on the way in and up on the way home.

Being up the hill is why the mornings are quiet, and being quiet is why the plan at the top of this page starts at half past six and works.

If you are staying somewhere else, all of the above still holds. We would rather you had a good two days in Ella than a bad two days in Ella, and we are not going to pretend that this page was written for any other reason than that some of the people who read it will want a bed.
